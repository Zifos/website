---
title: "The legend of the fire"
---

They say that, many years ago, three youths met where everything was ablaze.

Those were days of running toward the smoke, of defying the flames and of trusting, blindly, whoever walked beside you.

They had more dreams than certainties, and the courage of those who have yet to learn their limits.

At nightfall, covered in soot and worn out by the day, they would return to a small clearing at the foot of the mountains.

There, they lit a fire.

Between toasts and brews, they shared their wins, their stumbles and those dreams they didn't yet dare to say out loud. It was curious that, after spending their days fighting flames, they found their refuge in one.

Between the warmth of the flames and the dust of the ashes, something took shape that none of them ever needed to put into words. A trust without reservations, an affection without conditions, a complicity recognized in a single glance.

Beyond the valley rose mountains none of them had explored. And in time, the desire to know what lay behind them grew too great to ignore.

The first to leave followed the call of distant lands, crossing the known borders toward places where even the stars seemed to sit elsewhere in the sky.

The second followed the course of the valleys. From shelter to shelter, from horizon to horizon, along roads that, many years later, would lead back home.

The third stayed by the fire a while longer. Until he, too, felt the call of something that had no shape yet, and set out with the patience of a craftsman and hands eager to bring it to life.

The old clearing fell silent.

Where a fire once burned, only a small circle of stones remained.

The years went by.

They crossed remote lands, endured endless winters and fought battles the others would never come to know.

Some castles rose; others were reduced to rubble. There were unexpected conquests, dead-end roads and nights when there was barely enough firewood to keep going.

Each of them learned to light their own fire.

Some nights, under strange skies, the crackle of the embers brought back the memory of that small clearing at the foot of the mountains.

One afternoon like any other, the sun crossed the sky unhurried and began to set beyond the valley.

One of those travelers was walking an old trail, nearly erased by the years. Among the trees, something made them stop: roots wrapped around a circle of stones, blackened by time.

The traveler knelt beside the stones, brushed away the dry leaves and gathered a few branches.

Before night fell, a small flame lit up the clearing once more.

A few words, known long before they were ever written, crossed the distance.

Once again, there is fire in the clearing.

And they came.

They arrived from different horizons, with slower steps and heavy packs. They carried scars, stories and the fortune of everything they had lived.

They embraced, set their belongings down on the grass and took their old places around the fire.

Beneath an immense sky, they raised their glasses once more and told each other of their wanderings.

They spoke of the lands they had known, of battles lost, of small victories and of the peaks they still dreamed of reaching.

The night grew long. And at times, between the crackling of the wood and the silence of the mountains, it seemed as if the years had never passed.

Toward dawn, an icy wind swept down over the valley.

The flames wavered.

One went for more firewood. Another arranged the logs. The third gathered some stones to shield the embers.

No one said a word. Their hands remembered an ancient dance.

One gesture found its answer in the next. A stone found its place beside another. The wood yielded to the tools.

The wind died down. The hands kept going.

While one carved the stone, chisel blow by chisel blow, another poured hot water into a *mate*. The third traced shapes in the air with his fingers.

Amid stories and laughter, they worked to the rhythm of a music only they could hear.

The rough became delicate. The simple, beautiful.

And amid the stone dust, the scent of freshly carved wood and the crackling of the embers, almost without noticing, a fortress began to emerge.

Its walls embraced the fire. Its open gateways framed the trails that climbed toward the mountains.

By the time the sky began to take on the color of embers, the three of them stopped.

Exhausted, but full of life, they gazed in silence at what had taken shape around them.

From there, the mountains stretched as far as the eye could see.

There were peaks left to climb. Lands to discover. Castles not yet even imagined.

Before them, the world unfolded in untrodden trails their eyes were already walking.

Behind them, the fire kept burning, sheltered by the stones.

And as the sun set the peaks alight, the three exchanged a glance.

They smiled.

The morning had only just begun.
